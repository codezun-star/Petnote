Tennessee writes its rabies requirement backwards from how most states do it, and the difference is genuinely useful to understand.

Most states say: vaccinate by age X. Tennessee says: it is unlawful to keep an unvaccinated animal past age Y — and separately tells you the earliest age at which you may vaccinate.

## The two ages

Under [T.C.A. § 68-8-103](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-68/health/chapter-8/section-68-8-103/), it is unlawful for any person to own, keep or harbour any dog or cat **six months of age or older** that has not been vaccinated against rabies as required by the chapter.

And: dogs and cats may be vaccinated as early as **three months** of age, or at an age specified by the vaccine's USDA licence, but will be considered non-compliant if over six months without vaccination.

So there is a three-month window in which vaccination is permitted but not yet compulsory. For most owners the practical advice is unchanged — vaccinate when your veterinarian recommends it, usually well inside that window — but the legal deadline is the six-month mark, and that is the date that matters if you are adopting an older puppy or bringing an animal in from another state.

All rabies vaccinations required by the chapter must be administered only by or under the supervision of a veterinarian. And the statute contains a sensible limiting clause: nothing in the section is to be construed to require more frequent vaccination, or a greater number of vaccinations, than the rabies compendium requires.

## Registration runs through the certificate

[T.C.A. § 68-8-104](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-68/health/chapter-8/section-68-8-104/) closes the loop: **no dog or cat registration certificate shall be issued unless an unexpired certificate of rabies vaccination is exhibited.**

"Unexpired" is doing real work in that sentence. It is not enough to have been vaccinated at some point; the certificate must still be current at the moment of registration. Which means knowing the expiry — which means knowing whether the product was one-year or three-year labelled.

## The number you cannot derive

Notice how often that comes up across state lines: the expiry date of a rabies vaccination is a function of the product used, and the product is recorded on a certificate you were handed once.

If you know only "she had her rabies shot last year," you cannot answer whether her certificate is unexpired. If you know "she had a three-year product in March 2025," you can.

This is the single most valuable thing to capture at the moment of vaccination, and the easiest to lose. The clinic knows. The certificate says. Your memory, six months later, does not.

## Tennessee's parasite pressure

Tennessee appears in American Heartworm Society reporting among the states with the highest heartworm case counts, alongside its Southeastern neighbours.

Tennessee's geography spreads that risk unevenly. The Mississippi bottomlands around Memphis are a different mosquito environment from the Cumberland Plateau or the mountains in the east, and a dog's exposure depends on where it actually spends its summer rather than which state is on its licence.

What that means for a record is the same as it means elsewhere, but with more urgency than a northern state: log the annual heartworm test with its result, and log the prevention doses with their dates. A monthly product is a monthly commitment, and the months it was skipped are precisely the months nobody writes down.

## Moving within a state that people move within

Tennessee's metro areas — Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville, Chattanooga — sit far apart, and moving between them means a new veterinary practice and a cold start on history. The animal's registration is local. Its history is not portable unless you make it so.

A file that travels holds:

- **The rabies certificate**, with the date, the product and therefore the expiry.
- **The registration certificate** and the jurisdiction that issued it.
- **Heartworm test dates and results.**
- **Prevention actually given**, with dates.
- **Chronic conditions, surgeries, medications and allergies**, which no registration authority holds.
