Washington's rabies rule contains a detail that most owners never think about, and it is not the vaccination requirement. It is the retention period.

## The requirement first

[WAC 246-100-197](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=246-100-197) requires an owner of a dog, cat or ferret to have the animal vaccinated against rabies and revaccinated following veterinary and vaccine manufacturer instructions. An "owner" is any person legally responsible for the care and actions of a pet animal.

This is a statewide rule, not a municipal ordinance that applies only in Seattle or Spokane, and the [Washington State Department of Health](https://doh.wa.gov/you-and-your-family/illness-and-disease-z/rabies/rabies-vaccination-requirements-dogs-cats-and-ferrets) publishes it as such. It does not apply to animal shelters. The vaccine must be administered by a licensed professional.

Rather than naming a fixed interval, the rule defers to the manufacturer's instructions and defines three statuses:

- **Currently vaccinated** — initially vaccinated and revaccinated following veterinary and USDA-licensed vaccine manufacturer instructions.
- **Overdue for vaccination** — has not received a booster.
- **Unvaccinated** — has never received a rabies vaccination.

That framing puts the burden on knowing what product was used. "Currently vaccinated" is not a status you can assert from memory; it is derived from a label.

## The three-year gap

Here is the part worth pausing on. The signed rabies certificate is part of the medical record, and records are kept for **three years from the last treatment or examination** of the patient.

A dog lives twelve to fifteen years. A cat frequently lives longer. Three years is a fraction of that.

The practical implications are easy to miss because they only surface later:

- You change practices in 2026. In 2030, the old clinic has no obligation to hold anything from your last visit there.
- A practice closes or is acquired. Records migrate, or they do not, and the retention obligation runs from the last visit rather than from forever.
- You need a vaccination history for a dog you have had for eight years, and the earliest years are simply gone.

None of this is a criticism of veterinary practices. Three years is a reasonable regulatory floor for a business. It is just not an archive, and owners routinely treat it as one.

## What that means for you

The conclusion is uncomfortable but simple: **you are your pet's long-term record keeper, and nobody else is.**

The clinic holds the current chart. The county holds a licence. The manufacturer's label determines the interval. The only party with an interest in the whole animal across its whole life is you.

## The Pacific Northwest angle

Washington's climate shapes the medical half of the record differently from the dry Southwest or the humid Southeast.

Mild, wet winters west of the Cascades mean fleas can persist far later into the year than owners expect, and the seasonal on-off pattern that works in a state with hard freezes works less well here. East of the Cascades the picture is different again — hotter, drier summers, colder winters, and a different set of outdoor exposures for dogs that work or hike.

That intrastate variation is a good reason to record what your veterinarian actually recommended and what you actually gave, rather than relying on a general rule. A dog in Bellingham and a dog in Yakima are living different years.

Washington is also a state where dogs do a lot: trail access is abundant, and dogs that hike encounter wildlife, water and ticks in combinations that suburban dogs do not. Injuries, exposures and the treatment that followed are exactly the sort of history that becomes relevant years later when a joint starts bothering an ageing dog.

## Building an archive that outlasts the clinic

- **The rabies certificate for every dose**, not just the current one. The sequence is what establishes "currently vaccinated" over time.
- **The product used**, since the rule defers to the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Every practice you have used**, with dates. Useful when requesting records before retention lapses.
- **Chronic conditions, surgeries and medications**, in a place that is not a specific building.
- **Weight over time**, which is the cheapest early-warning signal in veterinary medicine and the one most often lost between practices.

### One thing to do this week

If you have changed veterinary practices in the last three years, request your pet's records from the previous one now, while they are still obliged to have them. It is a short phone call, and it is the only window you get.
